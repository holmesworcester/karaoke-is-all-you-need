# Karaoke Is All You Need

Use the scripts, file formats and commands in the [project README](../../../README.md). If the skill was installed separately from the tools, locate or clone [the repository](https://github.com/holmesworcester/karaoke-is-all-you-need) and run its commands from the checkout. Work on each song in its own directory.

1. **Identify the recording.** Match the requested release, duration, arrangement and first vocal entrance. Listen to candidate instrumentals for watermarks, cues, alternate edits and drift. When an instrumental is unsuitable, run `scripts/separate.py` on the chosen master. Keep the vocal estimate for alignment and the stereo instrumental for singing.
2. **Prepare the transcript.** Use a line-timed LRC for the same edition. Check repeated hooks, contractions, ad-libs and empty instrumental rows against the voice. Record the lyric edition and the audio source. Use `scripts/align.py` on the reference vocal to obtain a first pass. Request `--raw-output` to retain confidence and alignment evidence. The bundled model works with English words.
3. **Review the timing by ear.** Play the vocal and watch the sweep through the first entrance, fast lines, repeats and final entrance. Use the model scores to select additional passages for inspection. Compare a second vocal estimate or cross-correlate recurring snippets if a line lands on an echo or jumps a repetition. Fix the affected word intervals against the audio clock. Verify the beginning, middle and end of an externally sourced backing to distinguish offset from tempo drift. The [demo analysis](../../../demo/lights-burn-dimmer/analysis/README.md) illustrates repairs specific to one recording.
   Check both ends of each cue. Mark a start at the first identifiable lead consonant or vowel. Mark the end where the sung vowel or final consonant releases. A speech recognizer can finish spelling a word while the singer is still holding it. Run `scripts/refine_releases.py VOCAL.wav timing.json releases.json --output proposed.json` on the isolated vocal. Inspect the sensitivity range, long holds, overlapping harmonies, and proposals that reach the following word. A repeated vowel or a reverb tail can mimic a continuous hold. Compare spectrograms and the source mix when the stem is ambiguous. For suspected global lateness, mark a sample of clear attacks across the recording without displaying the current cues, check the signed offsets, and repair isolated outliers separately. Verify the sweep remains in progress mid-hold, finishes at release, and lines up after a seek in each export.
   For a DJ set, fingerprint short sections against the specific released mixes. Fit local tempo and offset across multiple independent landmarks. Keep loops and overlapping transitions on the performance clock, and compare candidate releases against each other before listing two identifications of the same sample. A studio lyric sheet may omit bars or label a rearranged hook incorrectly. Check repeated phrases on the separated remix before transferring both endpoints. For a chopped synth lead, estimate the phonemes on the isolated recording. Check envelope valleys for repeated attacks on the same vowel and keep a continuous hold across pitch changes when the vowel has no new attack. Time the lyric and MIDI events independently on the same clock. Inspect the transition before the first confident fingerprint, then listen through the end for processed speech. Confirm the spoken cues appear in the rendered video. Check the separation in both stereo channels and compare the drum and bass transients against the source. When a clean backing phrase repeats, match its harmony and beat phase before using it to replace a contaminated passage.
   For overlapping voices, align each part on its own clock and give it a fixed display lane. Listen for short final words that forced alignment squeezed below the duration of one video frame. Measure the vowel attack and release on the recording, then check the encoded word at two positions within its hold. Use `scripts/check_sweep.py MOVIE SCORE.ass WORD START END --lane NAME --fonts assets` for outlined ASS lyrics with a lighter highlight.
4. **Build the requested presentation.** Run `scripts/art.py` with the title and cover, then `scripts/build-player.mjs` with the song manifest. Use the two preview lanes and native CD+G export. Mark a pickup row when a short pause makes the next entrance hard to hear. Give the native tile reveal and the palette ramp enough time to read. Use full-bleed artwork by default. For a cover with lettering that supplies its own title, use an artwork-only intro and preserve the lettering with framed artwork. Set the song’s fade duration to suit its instrumental lead-in and leave room for the first pickup. Use the lighter of the two lyric colors for the highlight sweep, including on light backgrounds. Compare relative luminance after CD+G palette quantization and check both colors against the background. On performance footage, outline both colors for visibility over changing scenes. For release cuts, identify the artist’s music video or official visualizer. Compare its soundtrack to the mastered recording across each edit. Use the selected release artwork wherever the video has no matching section. Keep the mix and all word times on the reviewed musical clock. Honor the singer’s choices about cues, graphics and format.
5. **Verify the deliverable.** Open the actual player. When a matching original is available, include it via `originalAudio` and use the audio switch to check the same lyric sweep in both modes. Seek while paused, switch during a word, and confirm both the position and the release hold. Seek both directions, pause mid-word, change speed, go fullscreen and play offline. Check the animated edge on a descender, a delayed pickup, the very last word and the artwork hold. For MP3+G, decode the exported packets. For MP4, run `video/export.mjs` and compare decoded frames and audio with independent seeks in the player. A browser sweep that matches its own timestamp table tests rendering; listening and waveform landmarks test the table against the singer.
6. **Hand off the requested files.** Give the user a direct playable file or link and identify the recording and lyric edition. Report concrete timing checks and passages that still need human judgment. Apply the visibility the user requested for the audio and demo.
